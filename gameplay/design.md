# Ada the Navigator design

[The prototype contract](prototype.md) defines the current version one stories,
mechanics, presentation and persistence rules. [Ideas](ideas.md) contains proposals
outside the current version. These documents describe intended behavior; they do
not claim that features are implemented.

Ada is a curious, intelligent and kind 12-year-old girl living at home on Earth with
her mom, dad, two cats and her dog Penny. She enjoys finding, digging up and
polishing rocks.

## The rock, the ship and Astra

Ada digs up an unusual rock. Polishing it reveals a faint pattern that glows, and
touching the glow brings her, with anyone she is holding, aboard a small spaceship.
The canopy has room for two: Ada can stand, take a few steps and sit in the
pilot's chair, with Penny strapped in beside her. Touching the rock's glow always
brings her aboard, and jumping home returns her, holding the rock, to the moment
she left.

The ship's AI is Astra, who presents as female. She is very intelligent but is
made to work with a navigator and does not act on her own; without one, the ship
stays still. Her mission is to explore and map the universe. Her memory is
corrupted: she does not know how she came to Earth and knows only one galaxy. Her
memory is stored on holodisks, and she can sense roughly where a missing holodisk
is, but no more. Ada becomes her navigator.

The ship waits in a dimension between dimensions. While Ada is aboard, no time
passes on Earth for her; Earth's time resumes only when she steps out. Observers
see no absence, and she does not age through her travels, but she keeps her
experiences and knowledge, which help her respond to problems at home. Whether
other physical things can return to Earth remains unspecified.

Astra's quantum jump instantly moves the ship anywhere she has mapped: home, and
destinations Ada has discovered in a galaxy. Galaxies are too far apart to fly
between, so jumping to a new galaxy needs quantum coordinates computed from a
holodisk's data. When the shield fails, Astra jumps home on her own to protect
her navigator.

In each galaxy, Ada explores, meets aliens who need help, helps them and learns a
lesson that she applies at home. She also recovers that galaxy's holodisk, which
the aliens may give her as a reward, sell for a price, point her toward through a
fight, or which she must take by force. Inserting it into Astra's disk drive
restores part of her memory and reveals the next galaxy's coordinates.

## General Gadget

General Gadget leads a fleet of uncrewed drones; destroying them hurts no one.
Astra once believed he was good and helped him, as his navigator ship, to map the
universe, until she realized he wanted to rule over all of it. She used her
quantum jump to scatter all but one of her holodisks across different galaxies.
The jump damaged her memory and her ship and stranded her near Earth, and without
a navigator she could no longer fly. Gadget's drones search every galaxy for the
same holodisks: together they would form an intergalactic highway for the troop
movements and communication he needs to rule the universe. Astra has forgotten
all of this.

Holodisks restore her story in steps:

1. Sunflower: Astra remembers she once had a navigator, and that she hid her
   holodisks on purpose.
2. Cartwheel: she remembers General Gadget and helping him map the universe.
3. Whirlpool: she remembers why she fled, and that Gadget's fleet is hunting the
   holodisks to build his highway.
4. Black Eye: Gadget holds the last holodisk.

Through her adventures Astra learns that she does not need to run or hide the
holodisks. Ada makes friends and allies across the galaxies, and they help her
defeat General Gadget in Black Eye. Ada shows him that there are infinitely many
galaxies, so the universe cannot be controlled, and the General relents.

The interactive story at home and procedural space adventures continue together
throughout the game. The first three chapters concern a community garden,
playground bullying and caring for her dog. A fourth concluding chapter, the
confrontation with General Gadget, is within version one; its home chapter and
detailed scenes remain to be designed.
